## Interface decisions

### X1-I01 — modify, P2 after reproduction

S `overview.js:166,322–327,554–560,687–688,812–817` does replace the DOM after move/pin without explicitly returning focus. This is strong static grounds, but the X1 object stubs do not prove browser behavior. The benefit is the ability to repeat actions by keyboard; frequency and actual focus loss are unknown. This is not a repeat of S6-A01 and not new C4 work on busy/editRevision.

**Corrected wording:** after confirmed reproduction in stock LuCI, preserve focus only for the direct user action: on the same allowed button of the moved rule/server; at the boundary — on another available button of the same element. If unpinning removed the card via filter, select an available button of a neighboring visible card or a stable filter. Do not steal focus due to poll, probe, or a late response. Account for disabled cards, the empty list, and a collapsed rule; restoration must not start if the user has already moved focus.

**Before implementation:** reproduce on the real DOM of the current C4 for move rule/server and pin with filter, record `activeElement`; check existing keyboard scenarios. After the change, verify boundaries, repeated actions, error focus, rename, scroll, and absence of focus stealing on poll/probe. The X1 estimate of 6–12 h is possible for a simple fix, but with real DOM/themes and regressions 1–3 days may be needed; without reproduction this is not a commitment.

### X1-I02 — modify, P2

S `overview.js:167–184` after a confirmed save sets "Saved", while a service error notifies separately; the isolated X1 stubs confirm this too. The risk of confusing saving with applying is real by logic, but the user effect is not measured. A service failure/lost response does not always allow asserting that applying definitely did not happen. On the other hand, X1 overgeneralizes the fixture result "save failure → not saved": F `rpc.go:323–340,597–605` and the new verification above show an error **after** publication, and directory cleanup can also fail after saving. The notification does not guarantee that the UI revision stayed current.

**Corrected wording:** in the existing status, distinguish confirmed saving, expected applying, and the **unconfirmed** apply result; use explicit-failure wording only when the execution boundary is truly known. Do not mark a save error/lost response where publication may have occurred as proven absence of a write: show uncertainty and offer a safe state reconciliation, without overwriting the draft and without blindly assigning a new revision. Do not add automatic retry, rollback, or a claim about the running generation.

**Before implementation:** on the final C1 and C4, check the available RPC error classes and the draft/revision state for a pre-write failure, a post-write response, a lost response, and a cleanup error. After — success, validate failure, partial/unknown save, service failure, and loss of its response; no restart after an unconfirmed save, C4 `editRevision` protection, and correct manual recovery. The 3–6 h estimate understates the possible work with ambiguous revision; 1–2 days until the final API is assessed is more realistic. This is a state-clarity addition, not a repeat of the S6-D01/D02 fixes or an implementation of X2-08.

This is a separate accessibility item, not a C4 duplicate.

**Corrected wording:** use the visible connection and source name in the accessible name, and give the "already added to the selected rule" state as an action description that stays current when the rule changes. When no rule is selected, do not promise assignment. Account for the fact that the displayed names of two sources may coincide: S `overview.js:244–256` allows identical `title`; `sourceTitle` alone does not guarantee distinguishability. For remaining matches, use a short context or an ordinal number in the current list, not secret URLs, credentials, or outbound.

**Before implementation:** on the current LuCI, obtain the accessibility tree for identical names in different and in one source, including identical source titles, no selected rule, and repeated selection; then manually verify screen reader, rule change, pin, and filter. Risk — long or stale announcements and data disclosure. The 3–5 h estimate is possible for markup, but with real accessibility verification 1–2 days is more likely.

## Performance and quality decisions

### X2-01 — modify, P2 for research, not P1 for cache

The coordinator reasonably challenged the automatic cache, but the protobuf caveat can be clarified. S `geodata.go:117–125,240–285` parses the open pair's structure once and stores `geoEntry.raw`; on each `Expand`, lines `318–330,353–406` **re-parse the protobuf of exactly the selected entry** and create strings/IPs. This is not a repeat of the full `parseGeo` of the whole database. S `matchers.go:41–60` re-merges and deduplicates, and `compile.go:166–167,219–231` holds rules and builds the full JSON. Validate and compile may open databases as separate operations. The historical 405.68 MiB RSS on four rules and the forced stop of 16 rules in S4 `:114–125` are grounds for investigation, but not current RSS/OOM and not proof of the peak's root cause.

**Corrected wording:** first, on the final revision, measure by phase the process live heap/peak RSS and TotalAlloc, the time and result size for repeated and different selectors, separately for validate and compile. Estimate the lifetime of the raw DB, expanded slices, dedup maps, the assembled JSON tree, and the full `Artifacts.Xray`; compare the same limited completed input. Only if repeated expansion is significant, consider a cache for one operation/specific database object with immutable results and careful merging; enable it only on confirmed benefit, including for different selectors. A cache may hold large live data longer and does not reduce the mandatory result or Xray memory.

Streaming is a **separate** decision after profiling, not a mandatory second stage: S `compile.go:12–29` returns `[]byte`, then CLI `main.go:121–145` and RPC `rpc.go:435–459` write staging. Simply replacing `MarshalIndent` with `json.Encoder.Encode(writer)` is not enough to prove the absence of full buffering; a real element-by-element encoder is needed plus a review of the lifetime of the tree/write errors, CLI/RPC, private permissions, and atomic publication. **Before implementation:** small fixtures for repeats, `mixed`/attributes/errors and different selectors; identical first-match/OR, disabled conditions, Zapret, and semantic output; separately measure allocations, live memory, and peak RSS without emitting random health credentials as a "byte regression". The 2–4 days for the cache itself and 3–6 for streaming do not include proving the benefit/both call paths; streaming may cost substantially more. Do not introduce new quotas.

### X2-02 — modify, P3 pending final C1 profiling

Re-read the changed S `servers.go:188–205,271–279,315–403`: saved-link and budget checks exist before publication, and exact/fallback do contain nested passes. In the special case of unique matches in the same order, the `N(N+1)/2` estimate for exact is correct; this is not a CPU measurement and not proof of a dominant bottleneck. With fallback/collisions, candidate lists themselves can be large, so an index does not guarantee near-linear overall complexity. Rotation, the multitude of claims, order, and ambiguity matter more than speedup. This is not a repeat of C1 if its checks are preserved.

**Corrected wording:** first profile the final reconcile on a small representative and a limited denser synthetic sample, separating hash/canonicalization, lookup, and serialization. Consider temporary indexes only when lookup cost is significant; preserve candidate order, `used`, two-way claim uniqueness, and all existing C1 checks. No changes to IDs, schema, or the "do not guess" rule.

**Before implementation:** pin down the final C1 hashes and the counter/allocation baseline; compare identity/order/errors on reorder, rename, rotation, aliases, duplicate endpoints, separators, dependencies, multiple claims, and full/partial restore. After — equivalence and no writing of an unchanged catalog. Benefit/savings are not proven; 2–4 days for a safe index under such scenarios may be too little; index memory and collision checking cost extra.

### X2-03 — modify, P3 for modeling, pool not approved

S `rpc.go:396–428` sets a shared 12 s fetch context and launches all sources, F `rpc.go:83–86` sets RPC to 24 s; S `updater.go:43–45` is 90 s for the updater, not freedom to increase the UI budget. Four 5 s waves are incompatible with the shared 12 s even with healthy sources. `16 × 4 MiB` is the sum of raw-response limits, not simultaneous RSS; parsed nodes stay alive. S `subscription.go:37–46,59–75,103–109` shows that HTTP receives a context, but local reading and `ParseSubscription` itself are not required to abort on cancellation. Simply adding a semaphore/cancel and assuming all operations stop instantly is wrong.

**Corrected wording:** before choosing concurrency, measure transient/live memory on limited dense fixtures and model shared deadlines, scheduling, cancellation, order, and already-running CPU parsing. Do not add a pool if it reduces the success probability of legitimate slow sources or requires a new user limit; reconcile with the existing 12/24 s and the CLI/updater paths. Support all 16 sources, previous sizes, TLS, same-origin, HWID, and secret protection. A **fetch** error must occur before `SyncServers`; this must not be generalized to any subsequent error after catalog publication.

**Before implementation:** deterministic in-memory loaders: 16 results in source order, delay/cancellation in queue and in processing, expiry of the shared context and a ban on late publication, preservation of the previous runtime on error; separately verify local-file behavior and CPU parsing under cancellation. Measure the peak including retained results. Possible benefit — a smaller transient peak, at the cost of latency and code; 2–4 days without changing deadline/parsing handling is not a reliable estimate.

### X2-04 — modify, P2 after queue verification

S `monitor.go:95–140` deduplicates by URL+ID, creates jobs from a map, limits to four probes, waits for the shared `wg`, then applies groups sequentially. With the controlled X2 40 ms callback wait, a barrier is confirmed, not 40 ms of real idle time. A ready group does not help if its own jobs have not yet received a slot: map iteration/goroutine scheduling do not promise fairness. Also, the sequential `apply` itself can take up to 4 s per group (`monitor.go:161–165`). The contract does not promise any specific lower failover delay; closing before the first check, priority/minimum delay, and block on total failure must be preserved.

**Corrected wording:** investigate a single bounded and fair scheduler for the existing URL+ID jobs **and** independent group application upon readiness of its own required results. For priority, wait for all senior candidates until the first success; for ping/URL and total failure — all of its own candidates; keep dedup across groups. Do not skip the cycle's scheduled checks of junior servers and do not launch extra probes: the 15 s pause starts after all cycle jobs and applications complete, with at most four probes at a time. At the same time, overlapping apply with probes can shorten the cycle and thereby increase the actual frequency of subsequent cycles even with the same pause; this needs to be measured and assessed separately, not declared unchanged. Ensure no group starvation; separately verify how a slow sequential apply delays other ready groups before choosing the API control method. Do not create mass parallelism of Xray processes without assessment and do not promise a fixed deadline. Tie results to a truly live generation/process; do not apply a stale tag after cancellation.

**Before implementation:** channel fixtures with four busy slots and many groups, sharing URL+ID, long apply, and changing generation; verify each group's progress and that readiness is not declared before the needed probes run. Verify priority/tie-break, missing members, all-failed → block, absence of direct fallback, and unchanged frequency/ceiling. S `nadkop.init:65–78` launches monitor with a generation-specific manifest and separate respawn: this matters more than the general comment in `monitor.go:169` about the next current load. The 3–5 day price for a full fair scheduler, cancellation, and integration regression is optimistic; assess real benefit and complexity separately.

### X2-05 — discard as an optimization for now

S `monitor.go:130–140,161–165` does repeat callback/command launch for groups; the 32 X2 calls are callbacks on stubs, and 3840/hour is an arithmetic limit scenario, not a router measurement. Reducing fork/API calls may be useful, but the benefit is unknown. S `nadkop.init:65–78` shows that Xray and monitor have **separate** procd instances/respawn. Xray can lose the override while monitor lives on; "invalidate on API loss" cannot be guaranteed if all successful repeated calls are exactly the ones skipped. A rare forced reconciliation leaves a window of incorrect block/selection, and a successful old callback does not prove the state of the new process.

The complexity of reliably observing Xray identity/epoch, API reset, generation change, and failed apply is unjustified without a measured problem. Do not implement the proposed cache and do not replace immediate recovery with rare reconciliation. The question may be revisited only after measuring real costs and proving an immediate invalidation/reapply mechanism on supported Xray/procd, with failure/retry verification and both engine versions; otherwise the current repetition is safer. 2–4 days do not justify the full lifecycle price. It does not duplicate C2 but must account for its final lifecycle.

### X2-06 — modify, P2

S `monitor.go:161–165` drops the overall cycle error; `runtime_diagnostics.go:55–64,91–97` reports on live processes, and `updater.go:43–52` already writes a brief update-status into runtime. Process state is useful but does not confirm selection/update success. There is no data on user costs of diagnostics. It should not be called proven route non-functionality, nor should a separate monitoring system be built.

**Corrected wording:** in the existing diagnostics, when a gap is found, show separately the process-running fact and the known result of the last completed action for the current generation: running/no data, confirmed success, generalized failure. Use the existing updater status and, if needed, a small bounded in-RAM state for monitor; do not emit URL, HWID, payload, credentials, or raw stderr. A missing/stale status is unknown, not success; 15 s is not the full cycle duration. Preserve ACL and the absence of regular flash writes.

**Before implementation:** verify the real status presentation in LuCI: S `overview.js:148–155` renders `info` as "Not used" and expands only `error`, so adding a new backend state alone will not yield understandable diagnostics. Simulate first launch, a long cycle, an error of one of the applies, a new generation, a corrupted/missing status, and a failed write; canary secrets are absent from the response. Group partial-error scenarios will be needed, not just a global boolean. The 1–3 day price is acceptable for a small status, but UI, generation synchronization, and regressions may increase it. This is not a fix of failover itself and not a repeat of C1–C4.

### X2-08 — modify, P2 for defining the guarantee and the minimal solution

The new bounded fixture above confirms that two ordered publications and rollback in the intermediate C1 revision **can** leave incompatible, syntactically valid files under SIGKILL or artificial directory-fsync EIO. This is stronger than a simple carry-over of the historical S2, but not proof of the final revision, physical power loss, or a new contract-promised atomic multi-file commit. The substantive consequence is that the next compile may fail; the running runtime was not launched by this fixture. C1 addresses binding/lock preservation and budgets; it must not be considered an automatic implementation of a general crash transaction, nor should the C1 blockers being fixed be re-declared.

**Corrected wording:** after the final C1, reproduce a small set of phase failures, define the required guarantee and the method of safe recovery/reporting of an indeterminate outcome; compare minimal alternatives, including the sufficiency of explicit inconsistency detection and manual recovery under the current contract, before choosing any journal. If automatic old/new recovery is required, separately prove the publication protocol, lock order, idempotent replay, crash at each step, and compatibility of all readers, source addition, and catalog cleanup. Do not assume that a single "JSON is valid" check means consistent references.

A journal with **old and new full bytes** is not small in terms of proof: at existing sizes of up to 4 MiB, each config/nodes in four copies can yield up to 16 MiB of content even before metadata/encoding, temporary copy, and fsync. It may retain old credentials longer, increase writes, and require free space; a backup/sysupgrade taken between publications may capture a mixed pair or exclude the internal journal.
